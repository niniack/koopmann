# Research notes from the night of 2026-11-10 (ways to make the modes and the architecture more meaningful)

Each lead: what the paper does, and what it would mean for the KAE. Only abstract-level claims were read unless noted.

## Leads that bear on the 2x2 blocks (size against angle)

1. Cadieu & Olshausen, "Learning Transformational Invariants from Natural Movies" (NeurIPS 2008)
   https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2008/hash/2e2c080d5490760af59d0baf5acbb84e-Abstract.html
   Complex sparse coding of video. The amplitude of a complex coefficient says WHAT is there (stable over time),
   the phase says HOW it is placed, and the phase SHIFT between frames is the transformation (motion).
   The two basis functions of a pair come out as a quadrature pair, so turning the phase moves the feature.
   For us: only a phase difference has meaning, never a phase by itself. The eigenvalue's angle is the phase shift
   over depth, so it gets a meaning once the block-6 phase is pinned (the `real_start` rule does this).

2. Berman, Naiman, Azencot, "Multifactor Sequential Disentanglement via Structured Koopman Autoencoders" (ICLR 2023)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17264 (code: github.com/azencot-group/SKD)
   A Koopman autoencoder with a loss on the spectrum: some eigenvalues are pulled to 1 ("static" factors, e.g. identity),
   the rest are kept away from 1 ("dynamic" factors, e.g. motion). Swapping the static or dynamic part between two
   sequences swaps that factor. This is the closest precedent for "imbue meaning through the eigenvalues".
   For us: a loss on the angle that sorts modes into types, e.g. angle near 0 = carried from block 6,
   angle away from 0 = made by blocks 7-12. Follow-up benchmark: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17313

3. Miyato, Koyama, Fukumizu, "Unsupervised Learning of Equivariant Structure from Sequences" (NeurIPS 2022)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05972
   An autoencoder whose latent step is linear; block-diagonalizing the step splits the latent into blocks that each
   answer to one factor, and a block's turning speed is that factor's frequency. Needs sequences of 3 or more steps,
   which we do not have (one step, block 6 to 12).

4. "Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders" (SASA, arXiv 2606.06333, preprint)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333
   An SAE whose units are small subspaces picked by top-k over groups, as multi-dimensional features (days, colours)
   get split over many units by a plain SAE. This is our TopK-over-blocks latent without the depth map.
   For the pitch: the closest SAE relative; what we add is K, which says what depth does to each block.

5. Erdogan & Lucic, "Group Equivariance Meets Mechanistic Interpretability: Equivariant Sparse Autoencoders"
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09432 (workshop paper, synthetic data)
   An SAE made invariant to a known transformation, plus one matrix per transformation that maps the code back.
   Same idea as our "an alteration is one complex factor per mode" test.

6. Keller & Welling, "Topographic VAEs learn Equivariant Capsules" (NeurIPS 2021)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01394
   Capsules whose activity "rolls" as the input transforms, learned from sequences.

## Leads that bear on naming or ordering the modes

7. Bhalla et al., "Interpreting CLIP with Sparse Linear Concept Embeddings (SpLiCE)" (NeurIPS 2024)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10376
   Writes a CLIP embedding as a sparse sum of word embeddings, without training. For the CLIP KAE this would give
   every mode a list of words automatically (decode the mode, pass it through CLIP's head, find the nearest words).

8. Bussmann et al., "Learning Multi-Level Features with Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoders" (ICML 2025)
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17547
   Nested dictionaries: the first n units must reconstruct alone, for several n. General features land first,
   specific ones later, and features stop splitting. For us: nested TopK would order modes from general to specific.

## Leads that bear on the "faster" prong

9. Baldock et al., "Deep Learning Through the Lens of Example Difficulty" (prediction depth), and the tuned lens
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09647 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112
   The depth at which a model's answer stops changing measures how hard an image is. Early-exit work gates on confidence.
   For us: the KAE can gate itself. If its answer is confident, stop after block 6 plus the KAE; if not, run blocks 7-12.

10. "Interpreting vision transformers via residual replacement model" (arXiv 2509.17401)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17401
    SAEs at every layer of a ViT, CLIP and DINOv2, chained into a replacement model. A competitor on "interpretable stand-in".

11. Gromov et al., "The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers" (ICLR 2025)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17887
    Picks which blocks of a network to delete by the ANGLE between the representation entering and leaving them:
    a small angle means the blocks barely change it. Up to about half the layers go before accuracy collapses (LLMs, with a short repair).
    For us: this is an accepted practical reading of "angle over depth", for the whole representation at once.
    The KAE gives the same quantity per mode: the eigenvalue's angle says which features blocks 7-12 rework and which they only rescale.

## 12. Discover-then-Name (DN-CBM) — Rao, Mahajan, Böhle, Schiele, ECCV 2024
https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14499 (code: github.com/neuroexplicit-saar/discover-then-name)
- Trains a sparse autoencoder on CLIP image features, then names every dictionary vector with the word whose CLIP text embedding is closest to it. No LLM, no human labelling. The named features are then used as a concept bottleneck.
- For the KAE: this is the scalable naming the user asked for ("meaningful across all/most of the features"). On the CLIP KAE, the change a mode makes to the output embedding can be named with words the same way. With the `real_start` rule a mode has two such changes: its kept part (the block-6 feature, rescaled) and its built part (what blocks 7-12 add). Naming both gives every mode a pair of words, "what it was at block 6" and "what depth made of it", and the eigenvalue's angle says how much weight the second word has.
- What it would take: a word list and CLIP's text encoder (both local). No training.

## 13. Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons (AKOrN) — Miyato, Löwe, Geiger, Welling, ICLR 2025 (oral)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13821 (code: github.com/autonomousvision/akorn)
- Replaces threshold units with oscillators whose phases pull on each other; units that describe the same object fall into step ("binding by synchrony").
- For the KAE: a published precedent for units that carry a phase, and a way to group modes: modes whose phases move together from image to image form a family. Untested here.

## 14. Polar prediction — Fiquet & Simoncelli, NeurIPS 2023
https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03432
- Pairs of coefficients are read as complex numbers. The next video frame is predicted by keeping each pair's amplitude and advancing its phase. Interpretable, and matches simple/complex cells in visual cortex.
- For the KAE: the same structure, with depth in place of time. It is the closest precedent for "amplitude is what the feature is, phase advance is what the next stage does", and it is a paper to cite for why a 2x2 block is a natural unit. They need two past frames to measure each pair's own phase speed; we have one (block 6), which is why our angle is one number per mode and not per image.

## 15. Into the Rabbit Hull — Fel, Wang, et al., ICLR 2026
https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08638
- A 32,000-feature sparse autoencoder on DINOv2: the largest interpretability study of a DINO model, and the direct competitor for "interpretable DINO features". Findings that match ours: a few dense features that are on for every image (our always-on modes), and pairs of opposite features forming signed axes.
- For the KAE: a signed axis is a 2x2 block seen along one line (phase 0 against 180 degrees); a block holds the whole circle, as our growth mode 331 does with image types. Their dictionary has no map across depth; ours does. They propose that activations are mixtures of archetypes ("Minkowski representation hypothesis"), which is another reading of a block's phase: a position between archetypes.

## 16. Transport operators — Culpepper & Olshausen 2009; Connor & Rozell; Fallah et al. 2023 (ManifoldCLR); MANGO 2024
https://redwood.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/culpepper2009learning.pdf , https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13544 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09542
- Learn a dictionary of Lie-group operators in a latent space; a transformation is the exponential of a sparse mix of generators.
- For the KAE: each 2x2 block is the simplest such operator (grow and turn), so K has a generator and a fractional power. K to the power t, for t between 0 and 1, draws a path from block 6 to block 12 for every mode with no extra training. That gives a test with a clear pass or fail: read the real blocks 7 to 11 through the KAE's encoder and see whether each mode's phase moves steadily from its block-6 value to its block-12 value. If it does, the angle is a clock for how far depth has got with that feature.

## 17. Block-Recurrent Dynamics in Vision Transformers — Jacobs, Fel, Hakim, Brondetta, Ba, Keller, ICLR 2026
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19941
- Treats a ViT's depth as a dynamical system run for 12 steps. A stand-in built from only 2 distinct blocks, applied over and over ("Raptor"), recovers 96% of DINOv2's ImageNet linear-probe accuracy. Along depth, token directions settle toward class-dependent "angular attractors" while norms keep growing, and the CLS token is sharply re-pointed in the last blocks.
- For the KAE: the nearest neighbour to our story, by a strong group, and worth citing as the framing ("depth is time"). Three differences to state. Raptor still runs 12 block applications, so it saves weights, not compute; the KAE replaces 6 blocks with one small net. Raptor's blocks are black boxes; K is a list of 1,025 numbers, each tied to a mode. And their picture of depth (norms grow, directions turn a little, CLS re-pointed late) is what our spectrum shows mode by mode: feature modes grow about 7.6 times and turn about 12 degrees, and a few always-on modes flip the average state.
- A test they suggest: their "self-correcting trajectories" could be checked on our modes by nudging a mode's phase at block 6 and seeing whether the real blocks 7-12 undo it.

## 18. Independent subspace analysis — Hyvärinen & Hoyer, Neural Computation 2000
https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1999/hash/148510031349642de5ca0c544f31b2ef-Abstract.html
- Groups units into small subspaces and makes the subspaces' NORMS sparse and independent, not the single units. On natural images the norm of a subspace becomes a feature that does not care about phase or small shifts (a "complex cell"), and the position inside the subspace carries the phase.
- For the KAE: TopK over the size of 2x2 blocks is this model's sparsity rule. It is the 25-year-old reference for "a 2-D block is a feature plus a free coordinate inside it", and it predicts what we measured on DINOv3: the phase at block 6 carries real information (about as much as the size).

## 19. Language Models Can Explain Visual Features via Steering — Ferrando et al., CVPR 2026
https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22593
- Names a vision feature by switching it on over a blank image and asking a vision-language model what it sees. Causal, and needs no example images.
- For the KAE: a second way to name modes at scale, and one that could name a mode's turned version separately from the mode. Needs a vision-language model built on the same encoder, so it is a later step than the word-matching of lead 12.

## 20. Temporal Sparse Autoencoders — Bhalla, Oesterling, et al., ICLR 2026 (oral)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05541 (code: github.com/AI4LIFE-GROUP/temporal-saes)
- Splits an SAE's latents into two groups and adds a contrastive loss that makes one group fire the same way on neighbouring tokens. That group ends up holding meaning (slow, shared across a passage), the other holds local syntax. Meaning is put into chosen latents by a loss, not hoped for.
- For the KAE: the same move with our 3 views of every training image in place of neighbouring tokens. Ask a mode's SIZE to be the same across the views of one image and leave its PHASE free. Size then means "what is in the picture" and phase "how this view shows it", for every mode at once, with no hand-made alterations. A search found nobody doing this for vision SAEs.
- It pulls against the `real_start` rule (which pins the block-6 phase to zero), so the two are alternative meanings for the phase: "what depth builds" against "what the view changes". A choice for the user. What can be measured today without training: how stable sizes and phases already are across the 3 views.

## 21. Symmetry-based disentanglement — Higgins et al. 2018; Quessard, Barrett, Clements, NeurIPS 2020
https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06991
- Defines a disentangled representation as one where each transformation of the world acts on its own small subspace, and learns such representations from sequences with rotation blocks as the building unit.
- For the KAE: the formal definition under which "each 2x2 block is one factor, and the operator turns it" counts as disentangled. A citation for the structure; their setting needs several transformations, ours has one (depth).

### 22. Jacobian SAEs (Farnik, Lawson, Houghton, Aitchison, ICML 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18147
Two TopK SAEs, one on an MLP's input and one on its output, trained with a penalty that makes the map between their features (the Jacobian) mostly zero. Shown on single MLP layers of language models.
For us: our K is the limit of that idea. Each mode at block 6 feeds exactly one mode at block 12, by construction, and the map covers six whole blocks. Their control is worth borrowing: they show the sparsity is much lower on a re-initialised transformer. A KAE on a random-weight ViT would show whether our spectrum says something about the trained model (not run; a decision).

### 23. Fourier amplitude = style, phase = content (domain generalisation line)
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09866 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04582 , PhaMa arXiv 2307.12622
In an image's Fourier transform, the amplitude holds domain-specific "style" and the phase holds the content that survives a domain shift. Used as augmentation: swap or perturb amplitudes, keep the phase.
For us: both of tonight's findings match this split inside our 2x2 blocks. Gray and sepia copies change a mode's size (80% of the latent change) and hardly its phase, and the phase is as stable across crops and flips as the size. So "size = how strongly / in what style, phase = what" is a reading that holds for most modes without extra training.

### 24. RotatE (Sun, Deng, Nie, Tang, ICLR 2019)
https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10197
Knowledge-graph embedding where a relation is a rotation of each complex coordinate: tail = head x relation, |relation| = 1. Angles get meanings: 0 or 180 degrees is a symmetric relation, conjugate angles are inverse relations, angles add when relations are chained.
For us: K is the same form per mode (block 12 = lambda x block 6). It is a precedent for reading meaning off the angles of a diagonal complex map, and "angles add when chained" is the depth-clock idea.

### 25. AdaIN (Huang & Belongie 2017) and TFS-ViT
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.06868 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.15698
The per-channel mean and standard deviation of a feature map carry an image's style; swapping them transfers style. TFS-ViT carries this to ViT tokens.
For us: two thirds of our state are exactly these statistics (patch mean, patch std). The inversion pilot agrees: with them the colours come out right, with the CLS alone the object comes out and the colours drift. It gives a label for every mode with no training: how much of a mode's push lands in the CLS (object) and how much in the patch statistics (style). Test: mode_parts.py.

### 26. RCDM (Bordes, Balestriero, Vincent, TMLR 2022)
https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2112.09164v1 , https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2022/bordes2022tmlr-high
A diffusion model conditioned on a self-supervised representation, built to show in pixels what the representation holds. Sampling several pictures from one representation shows what it keeps (object scale, gray against colour, background palette). They also edit the representation and look at the pictures.
For us: this is the direct precedent for "invert the KAE's output and edit modes", with a much stronger painter than Splice's one-image net. A diffusion decoder gives natural pictures and several samples per target, so what changes in every sample is the edit and what varies is what the state does not hold. It needs a decoder trained for our encoder (none found for DINOv3's CLS; see 28). A decision for later, not something to start tonight.

### 27. CARE (Gupta, Robinson, Lim, Villar, Jegelka, ICLR 2024)
https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2024/gupta2024iclr-structuring , code github.com/Sharut/CARE
A contrastive loss whose minima make an input augmentation act as a rotation of the embedding. Best when augmentations become small rotations. The embedding then stays sensitive to things like colour that plain contrastive learning throws away.
For us: the training-time route to "phase = how the view shows it". Tonight's test says our phase is not that as trained (it is as stable across crops as the size), so this meaning would have to be imposed with such a loss on the three views. It competes with `real_start` for the phase, so it is a choice between two meanings. Not started.

### 28. Decoders from DINO features to pixels
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14857 (diffusion on frozen DINOv2; the CLS-only variant "lacks high-level fidelity"), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11690 (representation autoencoders: DINOv2-B patch tokens to pixels), https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2511.20565 (DINO-Tok)
Published decoders work from patch tokens. From the CLS alone the pictures keep low-level look and lose the object.
For us: sets expectations for the inversion. Our state has no layout (CLS + patch mean + patch std), so pictures can show colour, texture and scene type and will not reproduce the photo. It agrees with the pilot.

### 29. Complex-valued sparse codes: a gap for transformers
https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/1312.4695 (complex sparse coding of sounds and image patches), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04144 (pairs of hidden units read as amplitude and phase under a slowness loss)
Older work pairs units into complex numbers: the amplitude says a feature is present, the phase says how it is transformed, and a slowness loss over video frames makes the phase move smoothly. The search found no complex-valued SAE on transformer or ViT activations.
For us: "the first sparse dictionary on a ViT whose features are complex numbers, with a per-feature map across depth" is open ground. The slowness-over-frames recipe is the same as the view-consistency idea (lead 20, 27) with views in place of frames.

### 30. Early exit with an accuracy floor (PCEE, ICML 2024)
https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/39668
Exit heads are overconfident, which spoils the compute-accuracy trade; PCEE sets the exit threshold from held-out data so that a wanted accuracy is guaranteed, on ViTs and ImageNet among others.
For us: the cascade (answer with the KAE when sure, run blocks 7-12 when not) needs the same calibration step, and their ViT ImageNet numbers are the comparison a reviewer will ask for. Our extra: signals from the spectrum (built share) that need no head.

### 31. Scope of SAE features in CLIP (arXiv 2604.05724) and PatchSAE (ICLR 2025)
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05724 , https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/3d5b603d631d595f56bc36b373458b27-Paper-Conference.pdf
SAE features of CLIP split by scope: some hold local, patch-level content, some hold global information (carried by a few high-norm patch tokens that the CLS attends to). PatchSAE labels patch-token features by class-level and dataset-level activity.
For us: the CLS share of a mode's push (mode_parts.py) is our version of a scope label, and we can add what they cannot: how the scope relates to the feature's map across depth (its eigenvalue).

### 32. Features by depth: Network Dissection and "What do Vision Transformers Learn?"
https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Bau_Network_Dissection_Quantifying_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf , https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06727
Early layers respond to colours and textures, late layers to objects; in ViTs the last layer drops layout and acts as a learned pooling.
For us: this is what the size of λ shows, one number per mode. On the DINOv3 run the spectrum has two groups: 134 modes that blocks 7-12 hardly amplify (size under 5) and that are not tied to the class (class explains 8% of their size), and 305 modes amplified 7 to 10 times that are (40%). The angle adds nothing to that once the size is held fixed (angle_vs_class.py).

### 33. Linear Recurrent Unit (Orvieto et al., ICML 2023)
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06349
A recurrent net whose recurrence is a diagonal of complex eigenvalues, each written in polar form (size and phase are separate parameters, λ = exp(-exp(ν) + i·exp(θ))), with its own start range for the phase. Small phases at the start help on very long sequences.
For us: a precedent for a complex diagonal map whose size and angle are separate parameters. Writing our eigenvalues this way would let a loss or a prior act on the angle alone (for example a pull to 0°, so a mode turns only if turning pays). A training change, so a decision.

### 34. Transformer Block Coupling (Aubry, Meng, Sugolov, Papyan, ICLR 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07810
The singular vectors of each block's linearized map line up across depth in 38 language models, the alignment grows with training and goes with better benchmark scores.
For us: support for the premise of the KAE, that one basis can serve six blocks at once (K is diagonal in one shared basis). They work with the model's own coordinates; we learn the basis.

### 35. Direction against magnitude at matched size (arXiv 2602.11169, Pythia language models; abstract read only)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11169
Turns and rescalings of a hidden state are compared at the same Euclidean displacement. They damage different things: turns hurt the language-model loss far more and are partly repaired by fixing attention; rescalings hurt subject-verb agreement and are repaired by fixing LayerNorm.
For us: the matched-displacement control is the fair way to compare a turn of a mode with a rescaling of it, and "do the two edits change different things" is a test that runs over every mode (matched_edits.py).

### 36. The Transformer as a Polar State Estimator (arXiv 2605.11007; abstract read only)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11007
Theory only: attention, residuals and normalization derived from estimating a state held in polar coordinates (radius and direction as separate variables). No experiments in the abstract.
For us: one more place where size and direction are treated as separate state variables; nothing to reuse yet.

### 37. Phasor codes (Fourier holographic reduced representations) and their generalization
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.09689 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12880
Symbols are vectors of unit complex numbers; binding two symbols multiplies them, which adds their phases, and unbinding multiplies by the conjugate.
For us: in these codes a phase shift means "bound to a role". If a mode's block-6 phase were pinned (real_start), the phase added by K could be read the same way: the mode's content bound to what blocks 7-12 did with it. A reading to test on the realstart run, not a result.

### 38. Skip transcoders (Paulo, Shabalin, Belrose, 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18823
A transcoder (sparse features that predict a layer's output from its input) with an added affine path from input to output; lower error at the same interpretability.
For us: K's split of a mode into a part that stays on its block-6 direction and a part at a right angle to it is a per-feature version of "passed on" against "computed". The search found no paper that states this split per feature, across several blocks, so it is open ground if `real_start` makes the split clean.

### 39. When concepts appear with depth: layer-wise SAEs on CLIP ViT-B/32 and "From Colors to Classes"
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05276 , https://www.themoonlight.io/review/from-colors-to-classes-emergence-of-concepts-in-vision-transformers , https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.22902.md
Per-layer SAEs and neuron labels show colours and textures early and classes late; a 2026 preprint contrasts transcoders (a sparse stand-in for what a layer computes) with SAEs (what a layer holds).
For us: these need one dictionary per layer and a matching step between layers. One KAE gives each feature both ends at once, and its eigenvalue says how the feature changed in between (size of λ: look modes against object modes).

### 40. HAKE: polar embeddings where the size is a level and the phase picks within a level (Zhang et al., AAAI 2020)
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09419
Knowledge-graph embedding in polar coordinates: the modulus places an entity at a level of a hierarchy, the phase tells apart entities at the same level; the ablation shows both parts are needed.
For us: the size of λ already acts as a level (look modes against object modes). Within a mode, the phase of z carries about as much of the answer as its size (phase to average costs .151 accuracy, size to average .167), which is the same division of labour one level down.

### 41. Capsules: length = is it there, orientation = which variant (Sabour, Frosst, Hinton 2017)
https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.09829
A capsule is a small vector; its length is the probability that the entity is present and its orientation holds the entity's properties.
For us: a precedent for reading each 2-number mode as "size = how much, direction = which variant". Our two growth modes on DINOv3 behave this way (the direction says the image type).

### 42. Monosemanticity score for vision features (Pach et al., NeurIPS 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02821 , https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/89e83382abeee53b932a6df62edbf9cc-Abstract-Conference.html
One number per feature, with no labels and no hand-made alterations: the similarity of pairs of images (in a separate image encoder), weighted by how strongly both images fire the feature. Checked against a user study. Raw CLIP neurons score low, SAE latents higher.
For us: a scalable measure that covers every mode. It lets us say "modes are as single-meaning as SAE latents" (or not) with a published yardstick, and compare the score of a mode at block 6 against block 12, and against the size and angle of λ. Cheap to compute from the caches (done tonight, see ms_score.py).

### 43. Universal SAEs: one dictionary shared by several vision models (Thasarathan et al., ICML 2025); SPARC
https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03714 , https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06265v1
One sparse dictionary encodes the activations of any of several models and decodes to any other; finds concepts shared across models and concepts unique to one.
For us (a decision, not started): one set of modes with one eigenvalue per model. The eigenvalue then compares models: "CLIP amplifies this cue 6 times and turns it, DINOv3 leaves it alone". That is a use of the eigenvalue that an SAE has no slot for.

### 44. Linear Spaces of Meanings (Trager et al., ICCV 2023)
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14383
In CLIP, the embedding of "attribute + object" is close to the sum of an attribute vector and an object vector ("ideal words").
For us: the cue-and-conclusion reading of a mode is the same split made inside one mode: kept part = the attribute seen early (snowy, stringy), built part = the object concluded late. On the control run this split does not appear by itself (built pushes are no more class-focused than kept pushes, 50% of modes); the real-start run is the test. Attribute-object datasets (MIT-States) would be the outside benchmark (a download, not started).

### 45. Sparse but Wrong: the number of active features decides what the features are (Chanin & Garriga-Alonso, 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16560
In toy models and LLM SAEs, a wrong number of active latents per input makes latents mix several true features; they give a proxy for finding the right number.
For us: every run so far keeps 128 of 1,024 modes per image, so a mode fires on 1 image in 8 by construction. That ratio was never varied. The monosemanticity score (lead 42) says our modes are far from single-meaning (CLIP control: median 3% of the way from "any two images" to "two images of one class"). A wider and sparser dictionary (for example 8,192 modes with 32 kept) is the obvious lever; it is a decision, not started.

### 46. Dense SAE Latents Are Features, Not Bugs (Sun, Stolfo, Engels, Wu, Rajamanoharan, Sachan, Tegmark; NeurIPS 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15679
Latents that fire on over 10% of inputs are real directions of the model, not training noise; they come in pairs pointing opposite ways that together rebuild one direction, and they hold things like position, entropy and principal components.
For us: in SAE vocabulary all of our modes are dense latents, and a signed 2-number mode is such an opposite pair made explicit. It is fair to pitch the KAE as a rotating coordinate system for the dense part of the computation; it is not fair to pitch each mode as one concept without making the dictionary sparser.

### 47. Spurious ImageNet and SpuFix (Neuhaus, Augustin, Boreiko, Hein; ICCV 2023)

### 48. Block-Sparse Featurizers (Fel, Kowal, Jacobs, ... Lubana, Geiger; arXiv June 2026) - the closest relative on the SAE side
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25234 , https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25234v1
(Read through a fetch helper: the first part verbatim, the rest as a summary, the last fifth not read. Check before quoting numbers.)
A sparse dictionary whose units are small blocks: keep the k blocks of largest norm (the rule our TopK uses), linear decoder. On DINOv3 ViT-B final-layer patches, blocks of 2 to 4 numbers describe activations more compactly than single directions. The block's norm says the concept is present; the position inside the block says which variant (cabbage morphing into cauliflower, curve orientation, scene luminance, cast shadow). They state that "the model selects subspaces and not a particular basis within them", so the axes inside a block are arbitrary and are fixed afterwards by PCA. Width 4,096 to 32,768 blocks with 1 to 32 kept. No step across layers, no transcoder, no Koopman operator.
For us:
- It backs the reading "size = is it there, direction in the plane = which variant", which our phase test confirms on the CLIP control (same-direction pairs of images look more alike than opposite pairs in 100% of modes).
- What sets the KAE apart: a step across six blocks. The eigenvalue then has a reading they have no slot for: the size of λ is the change of presence, the angle of λ is how far blocks 7 to 12 move an image along the mode's circle of variants. And the axes inside a block are not arbitrary for us: K fixes them up to a turn, and the real-start rule fixes the first axis as "what is already there at block 6".
- What they do that we do not: a linear decoder and a far wider, far sparser dictionary (at most 32 of at least 4,096 blocks; we keep 128 of 1,024). Both are levers tonight's measurements point to (a mode's write direction is not fixed under our MLP decoder; a mode fires on 1 image in 8 and scores low on the monosemanticity score). A "block-sparse KAE" (their featurizer at both ends, K in the middle) is the natural next architecture. A decision, not started.

### 49. Linearly-Recurrent Autoencoder Networks (Otto & Rowley, 2017/2019): what a nonlinear decoder costs

(The last clause is the search summary's inference from the architecture, not a quote.)
For us: our decoder is a 2x4096 MLP, so a mode has no fixed pattern in the state. ### 72. Code books and parent-child features (two readings of "a plane hosts several things")
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17230.pdf , https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tamkin24a.html (Codebook Features, Tamkin, Taufeeque, Goodman; ICML 2024) , https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118058 (A is for Absorption) , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17547 (Matryoshka SAEs, lead 8)

### 79. Why two linked snapshots can pin down features when one cannot (nonlinear ICA with temporal structure), and what stays free
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10930 , https://bethgelab.org/publication/2020_07_klindt/ (Klindt et al., Towards Nonlinear Disentanglement in Natural Data with Temporal Sparse Coding, ICLR 2021: a sparse prior on the change between adjacent observations, with a proof of identifiability; a secondary summary says "up to permutations and sign-flips", check the theorem), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.17882 (Identifiable Representation and Model Learning for Latent Dynamic Systems), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12086 (Eigenvalue initialisation and regularisation for Koopman autoencoders), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05317 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13329

- Use for us (my own argument, to be checked by the user): this is a clean "what sets it apart from SAEs" point. ### 88. The phase of a DMD / Koopman mode is a free convention (outside support for the gauge argument of lead 79)

### 97. "Does a feature persist across layers or get rewritten?" is an open question in the SAE world (crosscoders, feature flow, multi-layer SAEs)
- Sparse crosscoders (Anthropic, 2024): https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/index.html
- fmxcoders, Factorized Masked Crosscoders (ICML 2026): https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/79305
- Analyze Feature Flow (Laptev, Balagansky et al., 2025): https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03032 , https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03032v3
- Multi-Layer SAEs: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04185v2
- From the search summaries (none read in full): a crosscoder is one dictionary read from and written to several layers, built because a feature that persists in the residual stream is otherwise learned again by every per-layer SAE. The ICML 2026 paper reports that standard crosscoders "largely fail at recovering cross-layer features": decoder norms look balanced across layers while each latent is in fact driven by one or two layers. Feature Flow matches SAE features layer to layer by the cosine of decoder columns and labels a feature as passed along, "newborn" in an MLP or attention module, or vanishing. Balagansky et al. are cited for "most features in the residual stream remain relatively unchanged across layers".
- Why it matters (my reading): this is the most natural outside meaning for the angle under the real-start rule. There the overlap loss makes cos(angle of λ) the share of a mode's block-6 pattern that is still present at block 12, so the angle is a per-mode "passed along (0°) against rewritten (90°)" dial, learned in one model in one run, where the SAE route needs one dictionary per layer plus a matching step. The size of λ then says how much the later blocks amplify the mode, the angle how much they replace it. Our in-KAE evidence: push_turn on the real-start run (rank correlation of |angle| with the persistence of the mode's state write, size and usage fixed: -.61; control -.12). The decoder-free check (angle_persist.py) tests it on the real model's states.
- What differs: they compare decoder directions of linear dictionaries; our decoder is an MLP, so persistence has to be measured on states or on what the mode writes.

### 98. Koopman eigenfunctions are closed under products and powers, so a learned eigenvalue is only as fixed as the chart (Applied Koopmanism and relatives)
- Budišić, Mohr, Mezić, Applied Koopmanism: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3164
- Korda & Mezić, Optimal construction of Koopman eigenfunctions: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08733
- Kvalheim & Revzen (uniqueness of principal eigenfunctions): https://www.birds.eecs.umich.edu/assets/pdf/2021-Kvalheim-MTNS.pdf
- On the algebra of Koopman eigenfunctions: https://www.themoonlight.io/review/on-the-algebra-of-koopman-eigenfunctions-and-on-some-of-their-infinities
- From the search summary: a product of eigenfunctions is an eigenfunction (eigenvalues multiply); for a real power p, φ^p has eigenvalue λ^p wherever φ does not vanish; "principal" eigenfunctions are a minimal set from which the rest is built, unique only under non-resonance and smoothness conditions.
- Why it matters: this is the textbook form of what the runs show. A nonlinear encoder can re-chart a mode (z to z·|z|^(p-1) changes |λ| to |λ|^p; a log-spiral re-chart shifts the angle) and K stays exactly linear. In our runs the isometric loss fixes the first freedom up to a recipe-wide level (sizes agree across seeds in rank, +.73; the level moves from about .55 to about 3.2 with weight decay) and nothing fixes the second (angles agree +.2, sign at chance). Cite when explaining why an eigenvalue angle needs a rule to mean something.

### 99. Rotation is the standard leftover freedom of latent dynamics models; fixes are anchors or structured dynamics

### 100. The overlap loss has relatives: kernel-alignment losses on an autoencoder's code (Deep Kernelized Autoencoder; CKA losses)
- Deep Kernelized Autoencoder: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07868 , https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.02526
- Patch-level kernel alignment (CKA loss steadier than a plain Gram loss): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05606
- Similarity-preserving distillation with a CKA loss: https://bmvc2022.mpi-inf.mpg.de/0535.pdf
- From the search summary: the kernelized autoencoder adds "the normalized Frobenius distance between the code matrix and the prior kernel" so that inner products of codes match a chosen kernel; later work finds a plain Gram-matrix loss "sensitive to distribution mismatch between the two spaces" and a centred, normalised (CKA) form more stable.
- Why it matters: our overlap loss (latent start-end inner products match state start-end inner products, each block centred, divided by the states' total) is this family, applied across two depths. Two things to take from it: (a) a name and prior art to cite for the rule; (b) the loss does not need the real-start restriction. Applied to ordinary two-number modes it would still pin cos(angle) per mode (the start-end inner product of a mode is |z|²·|λ|·cos(angle)) while keeping the phase of z free to pick the variant. ### Lead 179 (from the user, 11 Nov): Sparse Koopman Autoencoders Identify Local Dynamical Regimes in Multibasin Systems
